The big idea

Most people blame burnout on more work. The authors say the work itself hasn't grown that much. What's grown is everything you have to coordinate to get it done.

Collaborative footprint

How much and how often you have to work with other people to get your job done.

Microstress

Small, routine stress from colleagues: a vague request, a last-minute change, a decision made in a chat you weren't in. Minor alone, crushing together.

  • 50%+growth in the collaborative footprint over 15 years
  • 6–9channels teams use: email, Slack, texts, calls, meetings…
  • 5–6project teams per person, about 15 people each
  • 8–12%of a team's time saved by agreeing on norms

Four questions leaders should ask

Tap each one.

1Can we reduce structural complexity?

Flat and matrix structures mean juggling several bosses, and teams drown in uncoordinated requests. Companies keep adding complexity but rarely remove it.

Fix: give new teams and procedures a sunset date. Simplify on purpose, like Trader Joe's keeping its product count under 10% of the industry average.

2Does our workflow make sense?

Six to nine channels, each used differently. The real request is buried in paragraph 10, or a decision happens in a chat half the team never sees.

Fix: agree on 3 habits to keep and 3 to stop. It can save a team 8–12% of its time.

3Have too many teams spiked microstress?

People sit on 5–6 project teams on top of their main one, so nobody has time to build trust.

Fix: fewer, smaller, steadier teams. One company found no one should switch teams in under 15 months.

4Have we built purpose into everyday work?

Work done through screens turns transactional. Purpose also comes from small moments, like building on a colleague's idea.

Fix: connect daily work to the goal, and use it to cut what doesn't matter. And check whether you're the one creating microstress.
